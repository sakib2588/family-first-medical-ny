# Content writing and AI-quality gate

Three independent course sources agree (MASTER_REPORT §2.4; B01 F17, B02 F14, B11 F10).

## Rules

1. **Never publish a raw AI draft.** Feed the model the business's real facts, voice and examples first.
2. **At least 20-30% human edit**, heaviest on the first third of the page.
3. **Banned-phrase list** in every content prompt and a final search of the draft. Seed list: "delve", "embark", "it's important to note", "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", "as we all know", "studies have shown", "look no further", "cutting-edge", "one-stop shop", "second to none", "top-notch", "we pride ourselves", "we're passionate about". Extend per client.
4. **Specific claims**: numbers, names, timeframes. Replace "fast service" with the real response time.
5. **Pace publishing.** 600 AI articles dumped on a fresh site never indexed the homepage (CV01, community call 32); drip 2-3 per day per topic and watch indexing.
6. AI detector targets the course used (ZeroGPT 60-70%) are a rough guide only; the human-edit rule matters more.

## Stacked prompt method (B06 F11)

Build every page prompt from layers: master style prompt, a **company backstory** generated once and injected everywhere, field-specific instructions with word counts, and example content. Use a small fixed set of tokens for CTAs, images and internal links so every page is consistent.

## Images

Real photos first. For gaps the course used Midjourney (`--style raw`, landscape, stylize 150-300) with faces in frame and the city in the prompt; Ideogram for logos and text. Do not present generated images of "our team" or "our work" as real. See [[red-lines]].

## Proof

Reviews shown must be real, attributed and unedited (trimming with "..." allowed). Never invent reviewers or testimonials (kit file 04 §4).
