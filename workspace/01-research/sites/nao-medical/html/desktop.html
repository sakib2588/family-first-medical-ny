Before you book

A service directory is a starting point. Confirm that the visit meets your needs, and bring the information your care team requires.

Choose care for your needs

Choose care according to the problem and how urgently you need help. Urgent care assesses non-emergency illnesses and minor injuries. Primary care covers prevention and continuing medical care. A virtual visit can address selected concerns when a physical examination or procedure is not needed. Specialty care may require a separate evaluation, referral or treatment plan.

How care works

The first step is to decide whether symptoms need immediate emergency care, a clinic assessment today or a planned visit. The service name alone does not determine that choice. Testing, vaccines, school forms and ongoing medication management have different preparation and scheduling needs.

Eligibility and service suitability

An unexplained symptom deserves assessment before a wellness treatment. Preventive care can include blood pressure review, recommended cancer screening, immunizations and risk discussions. Nutrition, behavioral health or specialty care may support a broader plan, but availability and eligibility must be confirmed for the selected service.

Tell us what you need

Describe your main concern when booking. If you need a medical exam, state whether it is for school, work, surgery, immigration or commercial driving. Bring the requesting organization's instructions. For an existing condition, ask whether the visit is for a new evaluation, a refill, a procedure or follow-up. These purposes may need different clinicians or appointments.

What to bring

Bring identification, medication and allergy information, insurance details and relevant records. For a form-driven appointment, bring the actual form, deadline and outside results. For a virtual visit, prepare a private place, working camera and microphone, your current location and a callback number.

When to seek emergency care

Call 911 for a life-threatening emergency. IV fluids, supplements, weight medicines and hormone treatments have risks and are not substitutes for diagnosing a new symptom. Compounded semaglutide combined with B12 is not an FDA-approved combination product. A clinician must review the specific product and your situation before treatment.

Results and follow-up

Ask which team will review pending results and who will handle continuing care. If a specialist referral is needed, ask how records will be transferred and what to do while waiting. Follow-up for hormone, sexual-health or chronic-disease concerns depends on the diagnosis and prescribed treatment.

Availability, costs and insurance

Confirm current appointments, age limits and service availability with the clinic. A directory does not establish stock, a procedure team, a specific specialist or a particular price. Ask about the exact visit and any laboratory, imaging, pharmacy or procedure charges before choosing a non-emergency service.

What does triage mean when I am choosing care?

Triage means assessing and sorting care needs to determine urgency and the appropriate place of treatment. It helps decide which problem needs attention first; it is not a complete diagnosis. Describe your symptoms and how they have changed when asking for guidance. Call 911 for a life-threatening emergency rather than waiting for a routine clinic appointment.

How do I choose urgent care versus a regular primary clinician?

Use a regular primary clinician for prevention, chronic conditions and a continuing treatment plan. Urgent care can assess a stable new illness or minor injury that needs prompt attention. It should not replace ongoing follow-up. Severe breathing trouble, concerning chest pain, stroke symptoms, loss of consciousness or a severe allergic reaction needs emergency care; call 911.
