Primary care

Primary care is a place to establish a continuing plan for your health. Visits can address preventive care, a new concern and ongoing conditions such as high blood pressure or diabetes. The clinician reviews your priorities and records, identifies what needs attention now and plans the next visit. Keeping a medication list and a record of outside care helps the team coordinate treatment.

How primary care works

An annual visit brings together your health history, measurements, examination and recommended preventive services. Screening looks for a problem before symptoms appear. Diagnostic testing investigates a symptom or abnormal result. These are different purposes and may have different billing rules. A large blood panel is not automatically useful or required for every adult.

Eligibility: Primary care

People seeking a usual clinician, preventive care or chronic-condition follow-up can discuss primary care. The right schedule depends on your conditions, treatment and risks. A new or worsening symptom may need an earlier problem-focused visit. Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, stroke symptoms or fainting require emergency assessment.

What to expect: Primary care

At a first visit, review current concerns, medical and family history, medicines, allergies, immunizations and previous screening. The clinician can discuss appropriate tests, treatment choices and referrals. For an ongoing condition, agree on goals, what to monitor and when to return. Ask which clinician will review outside results and whether a separate appointment is needed for a second issue.

Preparing for your appointment

Bring your medicine list, home measurements if requested, prior results, discharge summaries and specialist contact information. Write down your main concerns. Do not stop medicine or fast unless the team gives instructions for the specific visit or test. Tell the clinician if cost, side effects, travel or language needs make the plan difficult to follow.

Risks and when to seek help

Routine follow-up must not delay care for a serious new symptom. Medicines need a safety review before starting, renewing or changing them. A refill can depend on an examination, recent monitoring, records and the clinician's assessment. Do not change a prescription based only on an isolated laboratory flag.

Follow-up and treatment review

Leave with the next steps, result-contact plan and follow-up interval. Track referrals until the receiving practice confirms an appointment. Share emergency or specialist visits so your medication list stays current. For chronic conditions, ask what symptoms or home readings need a call sooner than the next scheduled appointment.

Appointments, costs and coverage

Confirm primary-care appointments at the chosen location. Urgent-care hours and walk-in access do not automatically apply to primary care. Ask about your exact insurance plan, preventive versus diagnostic billing, laboratory charges and self-pay options. A location listing does not establish that every service is available at every site.

Questions about your care

What can I use Nao Medical primary care for?

Primary care provides continuing care: preventive screening, assessment of new symptoms, medication review and monitoring of conditions such as high blood pressure or diabetes. The clinician agrees on priorities, selects necessary tests and sets a follow-up plan. A specialist may be needed for a complex or uncertain problem. Confirm the clinic's age range and service availability when booking.

Do you offer same-day or walk-in primary care? Can I book an annual physical or preventive visit here? Can I come in for a same-day primary care visit if I am sick?

Confirm today's appointment or walk-in availability with the selected clinic before traveling. Name the exact service, patient age and any form deadline so the team can check whether that visit can meet the need. An open clinic does not guarantee the required test, procedure or a same-day completed form. Ask the selected clinic about the required service before arrival.

Do you accept Medicaid, Medicare, and major insurance plans? Do all primary care locations accept the same insurance plans?

Check the exact insurance product, clinician, location and service with both the office and insurer. A company name, Medicaid or Medicare alone does not establish that every plan is in network. Ask about the visit and test or procedure separately, your copay or deductible, and any referral or authorization requirement. Coverage is not a guarantee of no charge.

Can I use Nao Medical if I need a regular PCP, not just a one-time visit? Can I switch to Nao Medical if I already have another primary care doctor or recent records?

Ask to establish ongoing primary care rather than only a one-time urgent visit. Confirm clinician availability, network and any PCP assignment requirement, and transfer relevant records with your consent. Agree on follow-up and result ownership. A same-day appointment does not automatically establish a continuing PCP relationship.

What if I do not have insurance?

Ask the selected clinic for the current itemized self-pay estimate for the exact visit and any test, medicine, procedure or form. Confirm what is included and what follow-up adds. Ask whether that service and location participate in assistance; sliding-fee eligibility uses income and family-size rules and is not automatic at every site. No older advertised price, free visit or discount can be guaranteed.

Can Nao Medical help manage high blood pressure, diabetes, cholesterol, or other chronic conditions?

Primary care can provide continuing monitoring and treatment review for conditions such as high blood pressure, diabetes or high cholesterol. Bring prior results and medicines, then agree on goals, necessary tests and the next review. Complex, uncontrolled or uncertain problems may need a specialist. Confirm the actual clinician and continuing-care pathway at the selected site.

Do you offer bloodwork, vaccines, and screening follow-up through primary care?

Primary care can review which blood tests and vaccines are appropriate and plan follow-up of screening results. Confirm the exact collection and vaccine services at the selected site; not every test or vaccine is available at every visit. Ask who reviews the report and whether tests, vaccines or follow-up have separate charges.

What should I bring to my first primary care appointment?

Bring a symptom timeline, medicine and allergy list, prior relevant reports, any written test order or required form, and plan details if using insurance. State the deadline for any document. Ask about preparation for the actual ordered test; do not fast or stop medicines without specific instructions.

Can Nao Medical order referrals or help me coordinate next steps if I need a specialist?

Discuss the concern with the primary clinician, who can decide whether a specialist or further testing is needed. Ask where the referral goes, which records are sent and who manages symptoms while you wait. Confirm the receiving practice accepted the referral and check plan authorization; a referral does not guarantee an appointment or coverage.

Can I do a virtual follow-up after an in-person primary care visit?

A virtual follow-up can review selected results, symptom progress or medicine response if an examination is not needed. Agree on which clinician owns the follow-up, when it occurs and what symptoms require earlier or in-person review. New or worsening symptoms may change the plan.

Can I choose the primary care location that best fits my commute or workday?

Choose a practical location after confirming clinician availability, hours, plan network and the services you need. Ask whether that clinician can provide ongoing primary care and how records and follow-up will be coordinated if you use more than one site. A convenient address does not establish automatic PCP assignment or identical service menus.
